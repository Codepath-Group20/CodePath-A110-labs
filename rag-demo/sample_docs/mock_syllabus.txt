COURSE DESCRIPTION
================================================================================

An introduction to the fundamental concepts of computer science and programming.
Topics include problem solving, algorithm design, basic data structures, and
programming in Python. No prior programming experience is required. This course
is appropriate for students of all majors seeking foundational computing skills,
as well as CS majors beginning their degree program.

================================================================================
LEARNING OBJECTIVES
================================================================================

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Write, test, and debug programs in Python.
  2. Apply fundamental problem-solving and algorithmic thinking strategies.
  3. Understand and use basic data structures: lists, dictionaries, and tuples.
  4. Explain core CS concepts including variables, control flow, functions,
     and recursion.
  5. Read and interpret simple programs written by others.
  6. Collaborate on small programming projects and communicate technical ideas
     clearly.

Programming Labs:
Labs are short, in-class coding exercises completed individually. Labs are
submitted through the course portal by 11:59 PM on the day of class. Late
labs are not accepted, but the lowest lab score is dropped.

Homework Assignments:
Homework assignments involve more substantial programming tasks completed
outside of class. Assignments are due by 11:59 PM on the listed due date.
Late submissions lose 10% per day, up to a maximum of 3 days late.

Exams:
The midterm and final exams are closed-book and closed-notes. Students may
use one hand-written or typed and printed reference sheet (front and back, 8.5" x 11"). Exams
must be taken at the scheduled time; makeup exams are available only in
documented emergencies with prior notice to the instructor.

COURSE POLICIES
================================================================================

Attendance:
Regular attendance is expected. Because this is an accelerated summer course,
missing more than 3 class sessions may result in a grade penalty. Students 
arriving after class begins may or may not be admitted, at the instructor's 
discretion. Students are responsible for all material covered in class 
regardless of attendance.

Academic Integrity:
All submitted work must be your own unless otherwise specified. Collaboration
is encouraged for learning purposes, but submitted code must be written
independently. Use of AI code-generation tools to complete assignments is not
permitted unless explicitly approved by the instructor. Violations of College
University's Academic Integrity Policy will be reported to the Dean of Students.
The full policy is available at https://integrity.example.edu.

Accessibility and Accommodations:
Students requiring accommodations should contact the Office of Accessibility
Services at accessibility@example.edu and provide documentation to the
instructor within the first week of class. College University is committed
to providing equal access to all students.

Communication:
The instructor will respond to emails within 48 hours on weekdays. For
urgent questions, please attend office hours. Course announcements will be
posted on the course website and sent via the College University email system.
Students are expected to check their @example.edu email daily. For difficulties 
with your @example.edu email, please contact it-help@example.edu.

Conduct:
Students are expected to treat all members of the class with respect. Please
silence mobile devices during class. Laptops and tablets may be used for
class-related activities only.
